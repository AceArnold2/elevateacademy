Online Safety & Responsible Technology Use

The foundation every Elevate student completes, whichever course they choose.

Enroll Register your interest

About this foundation

Young people spend a lot of time online, and the risks are real: scams, privacy leaks, cyberbullying, misinformation and unhealthy habits. Before students begin work involving online accounts, shared files or AI tools, they learn how to protect themselves and treat others well.

This is not a lecture series. Students work through realistic scenarios, such as spotting a phishing message or deciding how to respond to online harassment, and the safety lessons are reinforced throughout every course.

What students learn

  1. Online privacy and personal information

    What counts as personal information, how to protect it, using privacy settings, and respecting other people's privacy.

  2. Passwords and account security

    Strong, unique passwords, password managers, multi-factor authentication and spotting suspicious login requests.

  3. Cybersecurity and online scams

    Phishing, fake websites, suspicious links, impersonation, online fraud, safe downloads and how to report problems.

  4. Digital footprints and online reputation

    What a digital footprint is, why online content can last, social media privacy and building a positive reputation.

  5. Cyberbullying and digital citizenship

    Recognising cyberbullying, responding safely, reporting and documenting it, and communicating respectfully online.

  6. Device and network safety

    Updates, screen locks, public Wi-Fi, app permissions, malware awareness and backups.

  7. AI safety and responsible AI use

    AI mistakes and bias, checking AI answers, protecting personal information, academic honesty and being open about AI help.

  8. Copyright, plagiarism and digital ethics

    Copyright basics, giving credit, using images and resources legally and respecting other people's work.

  9. Healthy and balanced technology use

    Managing screen time, taking breaks, avoiding distractions and building healthy study routines.

What students will be able to do

  • Identify common online threats
  • Protect their accounts and personal information
  • Recognise suspicious messages and websites
  • Explain how digital footprints affect reputation
  • Respond appropriately to cyberbullying
  • Use AI tools responsibly
  • Respect privacy, copyright and academic integrity

How students are assessed

Students show what they know through practical scenarios, not just a written test. For example, they identify a phishing message, judge whether a fictional website is safe, decide what personal information should not be shared, check an AI-generated answer for accuracy and choose how to respond to a case of online harassment. A short refresher is provided when students start a new course or meet new technology risks.

Good to know

  • Part of every course: this foundation is compulsory for all students, regardless of age, course or experience.
  • Equipment: a computer or laptop with an internet connection.
  • Privacy: students practise in demonstration settings and never need to share real personal details.
